\subsection*{Protocol at a glance}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Study type:} Quantitative benchmarking of stochastic HPO/NAS under identical search spaces, budgets, and evaluation pipelines.

    \item \textbf{Core question:} Does \ProposedMethodName{} reach better objective values / Pareto fronts faster (in number of evaluations)
    than strong baselines?

    \item \textbf{Fairness controls:} Identical encoding, training recipe, budgets, seed policy, stopping rules, and parallelism policy.

    \item \textbf{Budgets:} At least three tiers (default \BudgetTiersText{} evaluations), plus optional \(\BmaxSymbol\) for oracle construction.

    \item \textbf{Metrics:} Fixed-budget regret and fixed-target time-to-threshold; for multiobjective settings, hypervolume and
    \(\epsilon\)-indicator plus Pareto recovery time. Runtime is measured as \#evaluations (\#trained models).

    \item \textbf{Baselines:} Random Search; Bayesian Optimization (BO) variants including TPE (Tree-structured Parzen Estimator)
    and SMAC (Sequential Model-based Algorithm Configuration); CMA-ES (Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy);
    Hyperband/ASHA (Asynchronous Successive Halving Algorithm; when multi-fidelity is allowed); NSGA-Net (evolutionary Pareto baseline when applicable);
    vanilla \BackboneName{}; and domain-specific state-of-the-art methods where feasible.

    \item \textbf{Statistics:} Pre-registered nonparametric tests with multiple-comparisons control and effect sizes; robustness reported via
    median/IQR and ECDFs (performance profiles).

    \item \textbf{Diagnostics:} Convergence curves vs.\ evaluations, Pareto-front progression, stability across seeds, compute--performance trade-offs,
    and failure mode analysis.

    \item \textbf{Reproducibility:} Exact reproducibility for deterministic/tabular benchmarks; ``broad'' reproducibility for GPU training
    (documented nondeterminism), with full artifacts released.
\end{itemize}
