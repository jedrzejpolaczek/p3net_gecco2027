\begin{abstract}
Linkage-learning evolutionary algorithms such as P3 exploit dependencies between genotype variables, and
surrogate-assisted variants of them have succeeded on combinatorial benchmarks. We ask whether that
combination transfers to joint neural architecture and hyperparameter search under a small, fixed budget of
full evaluations. P3Net pairs P3 with a relative, linkage-aware surrogate that predicts the \emph{change} in
accuracy from a modification restricted to one linkage subset. We compare it against NSGANetV2, matched
engine and surrogate ablations, SH-EMOA, MO-BOHB, TPE, and random search on JAHS-Bench-201 (three datasets)
and NAS-HPO-Bench-II, at four budgets with 30 seeds each.

The result is negative. P3Net does not separate statistically from uniform random search in any of the
sixteen (search space, budget) cells, and neither does the same engine with an absolute surrogate. We trace
this to a structural property of the population pyramid: geometric level growth forces the uniform-random
bootstrap share of the budget to rise with budget, reaching 89.7--92.4\% at 350 evaluations even after a
warm-start redesign. Removing the hyperparameter dimension does not change the outcome, and a
reconstruction of the closest prior surrogate-assisted linkage-learning design reproduces the same null on
the same benchmark. We report the diagnosis, the fixes attempted, and why each fell short.
\end{abstract}
