\begin{abstract}
Joint neural architecture and hyperparameter search must trade prediction accuracy against a fixed, limited
budget of expensive full evaluations, and the field's dominant engine, NSGA-II-style crossover paired with an
absolute-regressor surrogate (as in NSGANetV2), varies genotype coordinates independently of whether they
actually interact. We propose P3Net, which instead couples the Parameter-less Population Pyramid (P3), a
linkage-tree-guided crossover operator with no separate mutation step, to a relative, linkage-aware surrogate
$\hat\delta_F$ that predicts the \emph{change} in accuracy from a proposed modification, restricted to the one
linkage subset that changed, rather than regressing absolute accuracy from the full encoding. We evaluate
P3Net against NSGANetV2, matched engine/surrogate ablations that isolate each design lever, and four further
baselines (SH-EMOA, MO-BOHB, TPE, random search) on JAHS-Bench-201 (CIFAR-10, Colorectal-Histology,
Fashion-MNIST) and NAS-HPO-Bench-II, across four evaluation budgets and $R=30$ seeds per cell, using
hypervolume relative to the best-known front.

The result is a largely negative, but diagnostically rigorous, finding: P3Net does not separate statistically
from uniform random search in any of the sixteen (search-space, budget) cells tested. We trace a substantial
part of this to a structural pathology in \texttt{Pyramid}'s geometric level-growth rule, which we show
forces an increasing share of the evaluation budget into uniform-random population bootstrap as the budget
grows (up to $93$--$94\%$ at the largest budget tested); a warm-start and hypervolume-contribution redesign
measurably, but only partially, corrects this. Once a population-engine confound in the matched
P3-plus-absolute-regressor ablation is independently identified and closed, that ablation also shows no
measurable advantage for the relative surrogate over a simpler absolute one, and a targeted fix for
$\hat\delta_F$'s provably additive-only representational limit, though independently verified to add real
capacity, likewise shows no benefit at this benchmark scale. We report these as honest null results rather
than omitting or reframing them, alongside the one benchmark cell where P3Net does win significantly, and
argue from the P3/GOMEA literature that the mechanism under test is more likely to pay off on larger,
structurally richer search spaces than the four evaluated here.
\end{abstract}
