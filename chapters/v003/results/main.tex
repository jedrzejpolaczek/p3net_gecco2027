\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports surrogate quality, convergence under fixed evaluation budgets, Pareto front and
hypervolume comparisons, and a fixed-budget summary table (median, IQR, corrected $p$~values, effect sizes),
in that order. The experimental setup and protocol are detailed first.

\subsection*{Experimental Setup}

The protocol must not allow P3Net to be read as ``P3 plus Pareto filtering.'' It states explicitly which
selection mechanics NSGA-II contributes to the baseline, what P3 replaces (the variation operator and the
proposal distribution), and, separately from the choice of search engine, which surrogate variant is used
(linkage aware $\hat{\delta}_F$ vs.\ an absolute regressor), so that gains can be attributed to the correct
component rather than conflated.

\paragraph{Benchmark and search space.} P3Net targets the joint architecture and hyperparameter search
setting rather than an architecture only space. Rather than resolving the Category~1 vs.\ Category~2
question by picking a single benchmark, both are reported side by side, deliberately. The primary
benchmark is JAHS-Bench-201~\citep{bansal2022jahsbench}, a surrogate benchmark over a joint search space of
ten dimensions (six categorical architecture edges plus four hyperparameters, including continuous learning
rate and weight decay), fourteen when JAHS-Bench-201's four additional fidelity dimensions are counted
alongside it, per the source's own ``14-dimensional search and fidelity space'' figure. It queries a learned
surrogate over its full mixed categorical/continuous search space without live training, so no enumerable
oracle front exists (see Metrics, below). Unlike the initial grid, which evaluated only JAHS-Bench-201's
CIFAR-10 default with no recorded reason to prefer it over the benchmark's other two built-in datasets, the
grid reported here runs the full comparison across all three of JAHS-Bench-201's own datasets: CIFAR-10 (the
primary dataset, natural photographs), Colorectal-Histology (medical tissue imagery, a meaningfully
different visual domain at the same $32\times32$ scale), and Fashion-MNIST (grayscale, clothing categories).
This checks robustness across JAHS-Bench-201's own built-in dataset family -- the same shared cell-based
architecture search space and small image scale throughout -- not generalisation beyond that family (see
Limitations, Conclusions).
NAS-HPO-Bench-II~\citep{hirose2021nashpobenchii}, a fixed grid lookup table over combinations of
architecture and hyperparameters, is reported alongside it as the Category~1 counterpart: within its exhaustively tabulated range (up to 12 training epochs; Section~\ref{sec:related-work} confirms $r_K$ is fixed at this level, not the benchmark's separate 200-epoch surrogate extrapolation), an
exact oracle front is enumerable, enabling the Inverted Generational Distance plus (IGD+) on this benchmark specifically. Any result that holds on
NAS-HPO-Bench-II and consistently across all three JAHS-Bench-201 datasets is reported as the primary
finding; a result confined to one JAHS-Bench-201 dataset, or that appears only on the surrogate-benchmark
family and not on NAS-HPO-Bench-II's measured data, is reported as specific to that setting and not over
generalised.

\paragraph{Baselines.} NSGANetV2 (NSGA-II with an absolute regressor surrogate) as the primary baseline;
NSGA-Net (NSGA-II with no surrogate, every candidate fully evaluated) as the engine-only ablation on the
NSGA-II side, symmetric to the P3-only ablation below; P3 alone, without any surrogate, as the engine-only
ablation on the P3 side; P3 with an absolute regressor surrogate (in the style of NSGANetV2) as an ablation
isolating only the surrogate, separating the contribution of the linkage aware design from the contribution
of the P3 engine itself; SH-EMOA (a multi objective evolutionary algorithm built on SMS-EMOA) and
Multi-Objective Bayesian Optimization Hyperband (MO-BOHB), the baselines established for this exact joint
setting on these same two benchmarks~\citep{guerreroviu2021bagofbaselines}, included because, unlike
NSGANetV2, they were never imported from an architecture only setting and require no such extension; random
search and the Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) as sanity baselines.
This grid separates the engine and surrogate contributions as far as the design permits, not as a full
orthogonal factorial: the relative, linkage aware surrogate $\hat{\delta}_F$ is defined only given a linkage
tree, so an NSGA-II-plus-relative-surrogate cell is not constructible, and any gap attributed to ``the
surrogate'' via the P3-only vs.\ P3Net comparison should be read against this asymmetry.

Without a surrogate, P3 alone follows the canonical optimal-mixing acceptance rule: every proposed
modification within a sweep is gated by a \emph{real} evaluation of $f_1$, exactly as step~3 of the P3Net
loop (Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}) would be if $\hat{\delta}_F$ were replaced by $f_1$ itself, and as in the original
P3 algorithm's own hill-climbing and cross-level mixing steps~\citep{goldman2014parameterless}. Under the
$\{50,\, 100,\, 200,\, 350\}$-evaluation budgets used here (Budgets, below), this is expected to exhaust most or
all of the budget within a small number of sweeps: for a joint genotype with dozens of linkage subsets, a
single parent's full sweep alone can approach the smallest tier. This is not a flaw in the ablation but its
point: if P3 alone collapses under this budget, that result directly motivates pairing P3 with a cheap
surrogate in the first place, which is what the central question (Section~\ref{sec:introduction}) actually compares. We
report the number of full sweeps completed within budget for this ablation specifically, alongside its
hypervolume, so a weak result reads as budget starvation rather than as a failure of the search engine
itself.

P3-alone's own population size (10) is smaller than every other arm in the grid (NSGA-Net, NSGANetV2,
SH-EMOA, and P3+absolute-regressor all use 20), an unexamined confound between ``no surrogate'' and
``smaller population.'' Two further arms isolate it: P3-alone-pop20 (population size 20, matching every
other baseline exactly) and P3-alone-pop40 (population size 40, double). Both are the same P3-alone class
and acceptance rule, differing only in this one parameter, and both are compared against P3Net using the
same statistical plan as every other arm (below).

\paragraph{Surrogate error accumulation ($\kappa$ and acceptance threshold).} The main comparison above
fixes $\kappa$ at its default value, eLyMPuS's own $2\lceil\log_2(n)\rceil$-step bound (Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}),
and the step-3 acceptance rule at its default zero threshold ($\hat{\delta}_F \geq 0$). Both are swept
jointly in the sensitivity analysis: $\kappa$ over a small grid (e.g.\ $\{1,\, \lceil\log_2(n)\rceil,\,
2\lceil\log_2(n)\rceil,\, \infty\}$), and the acceptance threshold over a small margin grid (e.g.\ $\{0,\,
\epsilon,\, 2\epsilon\}$ for a fixed $\epsilon > 0$), with P3Net otherwise unchanged, reporting fixed budget
hypervolume and surrogate rank correlation as a function of both.

\paragraph{Fairness controls.} Identical genotype encoding, decoding function $D$, validity handling,
full evaluation budget, seed policy, stopping rule, and deduplication cache (Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}) across all
compared methods, so that no method's apparent efficiency benefits from infrastructure the others lack. In
particular, the discretisation of continuous hyperparameter coordinates (Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}) is applied
identically to every baseline: NSGA-II/NSGANetV2 search the same discretised $\Theta$ as P3Net rather than
the unconstrained real-valued encoding a joint-setting extension of NSGA-II/NSGANetV2 would otherwise use,
so that any gain is attributable to the search engine and surrogate rather than to a difference in genotype
resolution. This equalises resolution but is not necessarily neutral: discretisation removes the real-valued
crossover on $\Theta$ that such an extension would otherwise use, while costing P3 nothing, since P3 operates
on a categorical genotype regardless. A control baseline to bound this asymmetry empirically -- NSGANetV2 in
its native, unconstrained real-valued encoding of $\Theta$ -- was planned but not completed for this grid
(implementation status: \texttt{not\_yet\_implemented} in \texttt{configs/methods/nsganetv2\_continuous.yaml},
skipped by \texttt{run\_grid.py}), so this asymmetry is acknowledged but not yet empirically bounded; running
it is left as future work.

\paragraph{Budgets, seeds, stopping.} Four full evaluation budget tiers, $\{50,\, 100,\, 200,\, 350\}$ full
evaluations, each roughly doubling the previous one so convergence trends read on a natural, geometric
scale. Each tier is anchored to a figure actually used in the surrounding literature rather than chosen
arbitrarily: the second tier matches JAHS-Bench-201's own suggested evaluation protocol of approximately 100
full evaluations, with a minimum of 10 seeds~\citep{bansal2022jahsbench}; the top tier (350) matches exactly
the full evaluation count NSGANetV2 itself uses to reach its reported results~\citep{lu2020nsganetv2}, so no
tier exceeds the regime in which the primary baseline was originally shown to work well. This also keeps
every tier below NAS-HPO-Bench-II's own convention of 500 trials for benchmarking search
algorithms~\citep{hirose2021nashpobenchii}, a deliberate choice, not an oversight: the central question
concerns a \emph{limited} budget (Section~\ref{sec:introduction}), so all four tiers stress-test a regime tighter than either
benchmark's own default, on both benchmarks alike. The 350 tier was added after an initial $R=10$,
$\{50,100,200\}$ pass showed several comparisons with a large effect size that did not reach significance, and every comparison at
budget~50 was non-significant despite visibly tight convergence-plot clustering; both observations motivated
checking whether gaps kept widening past budget~200 and whether more seeds would resolve the underpowered
cases, respectively. $R = 30$ independent runs per method with a fixed, published
seed list (up from the initial pass's $R=10$, still $\geq$ JAHS-Bench-201's recommended
minimum~\citep{bansal2022jahsbench}); identical
initialization scheme across methods; a stopping rule covering both the evaluation budget and, if the
search space is small enough to converge prematurely, an exploration collapse criterion analogous to the
one Bartnik~\citep{bartnik2026evolutionary} needed to add for spaces at the scale of NAS-Bench-201.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Fixed budget hypervolume and, only if the search space admits an enumerable oracle
front, IGD+; otherwise hypervolume relative to the best known front is used in its place, defined as the union of all points evaluated by any compared method across all runs and seeds (implemented in \texttt{p3net.metrics.hypervolume}). Surrogate quality
is reported separately as rank correlation (or pairwise comparison accuracy, if the surrogate is relational)
between predicted and true values as a function of $|\mathcal{H}_t|$.

\paragraph{Statistical plan.} The comparison set is P3Net against each of the other ten arms (Baselines,
above, including the two P3-alone population-size variants), per benchmark and per budget tier, not every
pairwise combination among all arms. Paired Wilcoxon
signed rank tests are run per comparison in this set, with Holm--Bonferroni correction applied across it,
and an effect size (e.g.\ Cliff's delta) reported alongside every corrected $p$ value. This is stricter than the uncorrected comparisons in the closest prior
work~\citep{bartnik2026evolutionary}, which report no effect size, and the difference is treated as a
deliberate methodological improvement, not an oversight to reconcile.

\paragraph{Diagnostics.} Genotype duplication rate and archive turnover over the course of the search,
motivated directly by the genotype-to-phenotype redundancy documented in NSGA-Net (Section~\ref{sec:introduction})~\citep{lu2019nsganet},
measured under the identical joint genotype encoding fixed for every compared method (Fairness controls),
so that any difference in duplication rate reflects the search operator rather than a difference in
encoding. The deduplication cache (Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}) is applied identically to every compared method as
part of the shared evaluation harness (Fairness controls), so the measured duplication rate reflects what
each search operator proposes, not an artifact of asymmetric caching. This rate is counted at proposal
time, for every candidate a search operator generates, whether or not it is later intercepted by the cache,
not at evaluation time, so it remains informative even though the cache itself suppresses re-evaluation of
anything it counts. Convergence curves; Pareto front
progression; stability across seeds.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Every cached evaluation is keyed by genotype, experiment type, and a protocol
version string covering every setting that affects the recorded value, so that a change in any of them
invalidates stale cache entries automatically, following the pattern used
by~\citep{bartnik2026evolutionary}.

\subsection*{Findings}

\paragraph{Surrogate quality.} Figure~\ref{fig:surrogate-quality} plots pairwise comparison accuracy
(\texttt{metrics/surrogate\_quality.py}: whether $\hat{\delta}_F$'s predicted delta sign agreed with the true
delta sign, once the scored modification was actually evaluated) against $|\mathcal{H}_t|$ at prediction
time, pooled across every P3Net run in the grid via \texttt{p3net.methods.p3net.P3Net}'s live
\texttt{surrogate\_quality\_log}. This metric needs a more precise reading than ``accuracy vs.\ chance''
suggests: a modification only ever reaches this log once it has cleared the step-3 acceptance gate
($\hat{\delta}_F$ predicting nonnegative improvement, Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}), so every logged
prediction is, by construction, a predicted improvement -- there is no possibility of a logged ``predicted
decline'' in this data, in either direction P3Net has ever been run. What the metric actually measures is
therefore the \emph{precision of that acceptance gate}: of the modifications the surrogate let through, what
fraction genuinely turned out to improve on their ancestor. Under the design finally adopted here (Design
decisions, below), that precision is $0.497$ pooled across every P3Net run in the grid ($n=7510$ scored
predictions, $|\mathcal{H}_t|$ from $2$ to $284$; across the six of ten equal-width history-size bins that
contain data, precision ranges $0.451$--$0.542$) -- indistinguishable from a coin flip on whether an
accepted modification actually helps. This is a real regression from what the pre-adoption default achieved
on the same measure ($0.672$ pooled, matching the $0.62$--$0.75$ range an earlier pass of this project
reported), and the Design decisions paragraph below reports what a dedicated investigation could and could
not establish about why.

\paragraph{Surrogate quality: magnitude calibration.} Figure~\ref{fig:surrogate-calibration} reports a
second, independent read on $\hat{\delta}_F$'s quality: not whether the predicted delta's \emph{sign}
agrees with the true delta's sign (above), but whether its \emph{magnitude} means anything, via
$R^2$ between predicted and true delta (\texttt{metrics.surrogate\_quality.calibration\_r2}). It is
dramatically negative in every search space -- $-51.34$ (CIFAR-10), $-30.71$ (Fashion-MNIST), $-12.88$
(Colorectal-Histology), $-1.64$ (NAS-HPO-Bench-II) -- markedly worse than trivially predicting the mean true
delta throughout, and notably \emph{not} in the same order as the epistasis measure that tracks the
ablation-family win pattern (Diagnostics: surrogate representational capacity, above): magnitude
calibration is worst on the search space where that pattern is weakest, not strongest. Binned by
$|\mathcal{H}_t|$ (the same binning as Figure~\ref{fig:surrogate-quality}), the badness is concentrated, not
uniform: early predictions ($|\mathcal{H}_t|=2$--$30$) are reasonably calibrated ($R^2$ up to $+0.17$), a
narrow window ($|\mathcal{H}_t|\approx30$--$58$) is catastrophic (as low as $-227$), with the single worst
prediction in \emph{every} search space landing at $|\mathcal{H}_t|=35$--$40$, and later bins recover to
merely poor. A follow-up check against telescoping chain depth (\texttt{p3net.surrogates.telescoping}: each
accepted chain step sums one more $\hat\delta_F$ call into the final estimate), on five fresh seeds against
the real NAS-HPO-Bench-II substrate, confirms this accumulation mechanism is real: squared error grows
monotonically with chain depth (median $12.96$ at depth~1 to $744.80$ at depth~5), and every one of the ten
single worst logged predictions has chain depth $\geq3$. Chain depth alone does not fully explain the
$|\mathcal{H}_t|\approx30$--$58$ window specifically, though: it is only mildly elevated there relative to
elsewhere (median $4.5$ vs.\ $4.0$).

A further check isolates the window to one specific pyramid level, not a general property of $|\mathcal{H}_t|$
or chain depth: pooling squared error by the sweeping level's own target size (same five seeds), levels of
size 2/4/8 sit at a median $67.8$--$117.6$, size 32/64/128 at $130.5$--$220.2$, but \textbf{size 16 alone
reaches a median of $1307.29$} -- $6$--$19\times$ every other size -- and is the level whose first
sweep-based predictions land at $|\mathcal{H}_t|\approx33$--$36$ in all five seeds, matching the window
above exactly; $9$ of the $10$ single worst logged predictions occur at this level size. A plausible but
unconfirmed mechanism: this level's linkage tree is built from its own population, freshly and
purely-randomly bootstrapped the moment this sweep phase begins, and if it proposes subsets not yet well
represented among the still-few ($\approx30$) matching pairs available for the one shared linear model to
fit, extrapolation for those coordinates follows directly -- not confirmed by measuring per-subset
matching-pair counts directly, left as the natural next step.

This failure mode -- a point-estimate surrogate trusted without regard to its own reliability -- has an
established remedy in the surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation literature: coupling model management
with explicit uncertainty (MSE/EI/LCB/PoI-style infill criteria over models that report their own
confidence) and trust-region or local-model restriction to avoid extrapolating far from training
data~\citep{jin2011surrogate}. The closest already-cited prior work facing the same class of problem,
CS-GOMEA, addresses it by moving to a strictly more expressive model (a CNN, also trained on pairwise
fitness differences, for the same evaluation-efficiency reason as $\hat\delta_F$) rather than regularising a
linear one~\citep{dushatskiy2019csgomea}. Ridge regression~\citep{hoerl1970ridge} is the standard, cheaper
first-line defence specifically for a small-sample, unstable, high-variance linear fit -- it targets the
symptom (extreme coefficient and prediction magnitude) directly, independent of whether the mechanism just
proposed is fully right, and is orthogonal to $\hat\delta_F$'s additive-only representational capacity
(Diagnostics: surrogate representational capacity, above): a different axis, not a substitute for that
finding's own candidate fix. Conclusions adopts it alongside the pyramid-transition redesign proposed there,
since both concern the same moment -- a level's first sweep pass immediately after its own bootstrap.

With \texttt{RidgeCV} adopted as \texttt{p3net}'s \texttt{model\_factory}, recomputed \texttt{calibration\_r2}
by pyramid level size at budget~350, pooled across all four search spaces, shows the pathology essentially
unchanged: level~16 remains by far the worst-calibrated level ($R^2=-4.71$, MSE $766.7$, $n=970$ predictions)
against every other level (best: level~2, $R^2=+0.55$, MSE $85.2$; every level from 8 upward is
calibration-negative). The level-16-to-best MSE ratio ($\approx9\times$) lands squarely inside the
$6$--$19\times$ range documented above -- regularisation, the textbook remedy for unstable small-sample
linear fits, does not visibly narrow this specific gap on real data, suggesting the mechanism proposed above
(sparse per-subset training pairs for a freshly-bootstrapped level, not merely unstable coefficients) may be
closer to the real cause. Left as an open point for follow-up surrogate diagnostics (Conclusions).

convergence plots (budgets 50, 100, and 200 on all four search spaces, and all four budgets on

\paragraph{Pareto front and hypervolume comparisons.} Figures~\ref{fig:pareto-p3net-jahs-350}
and~\ref{fig:pareto-p3alone-jahs-350} show the Pareto front recovered by P3Net and by the surrogate-free
P3-alone ablation, respectively, at 25/50/75/100\% of budget on JAHS-Bench-201 (CIFAR-10), budget 350 (axes
are independently auto-scaled per plot, so absolute extents are not directly comparable at a glance). Both
show the same qualitative knee shape, and both have their non-tail portion ($f_1 \lesssim 20$) settled well
before the 100\%-of-budget checkpoint. The visible difference is the flat tail: P3-alone's front extends to
$f_1 \approx 73$ (two points near $f_2 \approx 0.04$--$0.05$, both appearing only at the 100\%-of-budget
checkpoint), while P3Net's longest-$f_1$ point sits at $f_1 \approx 49$, also 100\%-of-budget-only.
P3-alone explores further into this high-$f_1$/low-$f_2$ corner of the space late in the budget than P3Net
does, even though P3Net's fixed-budget hypervolume is higher overall (Table~\ref{tab:fixed-budget-jahs}) --
a reminder that a single scalar hypervolume number can mask which parts of the trade-off surface a method
actually covers.

Across the full grid of eleven methods, four search spaces, and four budgets, P3Net's rank by median
hypervolume-relative-to-best-known-front is budget-dependent and, markedly, dataset-dependent -- not
uniformly high or low, and not consistent even across JAHS-Bench-201's own three datasets. On
Fashion-MNIST P3Net ranks 2nd-highest of eleven at budgets~50 and~100, then 4th and 5th at budgets~200
and~350 -- consistently upper-half, its strongest showing anywhere in the grid. On CIFAR-10 it opens
3rd-highest at budget~50 and settles to 6th--7th (squarely mid-pack) from budget~100 onward. On
Colorectal-Histology and NAS-HPO-Bench-II the pattern reverses: both open mid-pack at budget~50 (9th and
4th respectively) and drift toward the \emph{bottom} of the field as budget grows -- Colorectal-Histology to
10th-lowest of eleven at budget~350, NAS-HPO-Bench-II to 10th-lowest at budget~350 as well, having been
7th-lowest at budget~100. On none of the four search spaces does more budget translate into a stronger
relative position for P3Net. As the statistical test below shows, this is not uniformly inconclusive: 61 of
160 comparisons in this grid are statistically confirmed, and which comparisons those are tracks this same
per-dataset split closely -- P3Net's own surrogate-ablation family wins concentrate on Fashion-MNIST and
CIFAR-10, its losses to differently-engineered baselines concentrate everywhere, but its losses to its
\emph{own} ablation family concentrate on Colorectal-Histology and NAS-HPO-Bench-II. The raw ranking above
is reported plainly because it previews which comparisons those turn out to be.

\paragraph{Fixed-budget summary.} Table~\ref{tab:fixed-budget-jahs}, Table~\ref{tab:fixed-budget-colorectal},
Table~\ref{tab:fixed-budget-fashion}, and Table~\ref{tab:fixed-budget-nashpo} report median and IQR of the
primary metric, plus the Holm--Bonferroni-corrected Wilcoxon $p$-value and Cliff's $\delta$ for each of the
ten P3Net-vs.-baseline comparisons (Statistical plan, above -- including the two P3-alone population-size
variants), at each of the four budget tiers, on JAHS-Bench-201's three datasets and on NAS-HPO-Bench-II
(160 comparisons total across the sixteen search-space/budget cells, $R=30$ seeds each). Both still report
hypervolume-relative-to-pooled-best-known-front rather than the planned IGD+ for NAS-HPO-Bench-II (Metrics,
above); wiring in the oracle front remains open.

\textbf{61 of the 160 comparisons reject the null hypothesis} at $\alpha=0.05$. Thirty-seven favour the
baseline over P3Net; twenty-four favour P3Net. The composition of each side matters more than the raw count.
Of the 24 P3Net-favouring rejections, 23 are internal to the P3 engine's own surrogate-type/population
comparisons (P3-alone, P3-alone-pop20, P3-alone-pop40, or P3+absolute-regressor) -- exactly the family the
central question (Section~\ref{sec:introduction}) is staked on -- and only one is a win against a
differently-engineered baseline anywhere in the entire grid (MO-BOHB, Colorectal-Histology, budget~350,
$\delta=+0.322$, $p=0.038$). Of the 37 baseline-favouring rejections, 28 are losses to a differently-engineered
method (SH-EMOA, TPE, NSGA-Net, or NSGANetV2) and 9 are losses \emph{within} the P3-engine family itself --
cases where plain P3-alone, P3-alone-pop40, or the absolute-regressor variant beats P3Net's own relative
surrogate outright, concentrated on Colorectal-Histology and NAS-HPO-Bench-II (P3-alone-pop40 vs.\ P3Net,
Colorectal-Histology budget~350, $\delta=-0.529$, $p=0.005$; P3-alone vs.\ P3Net and P3-alone-pop40 vs.\
P3Net, both NAS-HPO-Bench-II budget~350, $\delta=-0.587$ and $-0.564$).

The core-mechanism comparison holds most cleanly on Fashion-MNIST: P3Net significantly beats plain P3-alone
and the population-matched P3-alone-pop20 at every one of the four budget tiers ($\delta$ from $+0.449$ to
$+0.791$), and additionally beats P3-alone-pop40 at three of four and the absolute-regressor variant at
three of four ($\delta$ up to $+0.676$) -- the largest and most consistent surrogate-vs.-no-surrogate margin
anywhere in the grid, and the only search space where P3Net also significantly outperforms
P3+absolute-regressor, isolating the relative surrogate's own contribution from the P3 engine's. On CIFAR-10
the same ablation wins hold but less completely: P3Net beats plain P3-alone at all four budgets and
P3-alone-pop20 at two (budgets~200 and~350), with no comparison against P3-alone-pop40 or
P3+absolute-regressor reaching significance in P3Net's favour anywhere, and P3+absolute-regressor actually
beating P3Net at budget~350 ($\delta=-0.491$). On Colorectal-Histology the win narrows further: P3Net beats
plain P3-alone at three of four budgets (not~350) and never beats either population-matched variant, while
P3-alone-pop40 beats \emph{it} at budget~350. On NAS-HPO-Bench-II the core-mechanism win disappears
entirely and inverts at the highest budget: no ablation comparison favours P3Net anywhere on this benchmark,
and at budget~350 both plain P3-alone and P3-alone-pop40 significantly beat it.

Put together: the mechanism the paper is actually about -- pairing P3 with the relative, linkage-aware
surrogate instead of running P3 with no surrogate at all, or with an absolute regressor -- has real,
statistically confirmed support that is strongest and most complete on Fashion-MNIST, still real but partial
on CIFAR-10, weaker on Colorectal-Histology, and absent (inverting at the largest budget) on
NAS-HPO-Bench-II, the one benchmark built from measured rather than surrogate-modelled data. That same
P3Net loses, consistently and with comparable or larger effect sizes, to several methods built on an
entirely different search engine (SH-EMOA, TPE, NSGA-Net, NSGANetV2) once the budget exceeds 50 evaluations,
on every search space. The central question's comparison against NSGANetV2 specifically -- P3's
dependency-aware search plus a relative surrogate vs.\ NSGA-II's crowding distance plus an absolute
regressor -- resolves against P3Net at budget~200 on three of four search spaces ($\delta=-0.444$ CIFAR-10,
$-0.818$ Colorectal-Histology, $-0.760$ NAS-HPO-Bench-II; not significant on Fashion-MNIST) and at
budget~350 on all four ($\delta=-0.413$, $-0.831$, $+0.320$ (not significant), $-0.940$ respectively --
Fashion-MNIST is the only search space where this comparison is not significant at the largest budget).

This attribution is complicated by a mechanism identified in a later pass (Diagnostics: population-pyramid
bootstrap share, below): P3Net is not statistically distinguishable from \texttt{random\_search} in any of
the sixteen search-space/budget cells, and its margin against every differently-engineered baseline tracks
that baseline's own margin against \texttt{random\_search} almost exactly. The losses to SH-EMOA, TPE,
NSGA-Net, and NSGANetV2 reported just above are real and correctly measured, but a substantial part of why
they widen with budget, and differ in size by dataset, is attributable to how much budget P3Net's own
population-pyramid growth rule spends on uniform-random bootstrap rather than to a comparative weakness of
the relative surrogate specifically -- see Diagnostics, below, for the full account and a proposed,
not-yet-implemented fix (Conclusions).

\paragraph{Design decisions: a P3Net ablation round.} Five further design points left open by
Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer} -- whether to compute $f_2$ fresh via the substrate's analytic cost
hook rather than inherit it from the ancestor, whether every pyramid level should stay live and sweep
simultaneously (cascade) rather than only the newest, whether population truncation should use nondominated
sort rather than a single-objective sort on $f_1$, whether stall recovery should force pyramid growth rather
than reinject random diversity, and whether the optimal-mixing donor pool should admit transient,
not-yet-evaluated individuals from the same iteration -- plus two of the algorithm's existing heuristic
defaults, $\kappa$ and the step-3 acceptance threshold (Chain depth ($\kappa$),
Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}), spot-checked at half/double $\kappa$ and a permissive/strict
threshold -- were each implemented as a single-axis variant of \texttt{p3net.yaml} and measured against it
across the same $R=30$, four-dataset, four-budget grid (nine variants, 144 comparisons total,
Holm--Bonferroni corrected per cell, per variant). Of these, one showed a consistent, partly-significant
improvement (analytic cost: favourable direction in 15 of 16 search-space/budget cells, significant on
Fashion-MNIST at budget~200, $p=0.0012$, and budget~350, $p=0.0034$, with a strengthening effect size and
tighter variance as budget grows, at no extra evaluation cost), and was adopted as the only behaviour --
\texttt{use\_analytic\_cost} is no longer a config flag; \texttt{build\_method} wires the substrate's own
\texttt{analytic\_cost\_objectives} into every \texttt{p3net} config unconditionally. Cascade showed one
significant win (Colorectal-Histology, budget~200, $p=0.0168$), a favourable direction on
Colorectal-Histology and NAS-HPO-Bench-II overall, but a neutral-to-negative direction on CIFAR-10 and a
non-significantly worse result on Fashion-MNIST at budget~50 -- combined with cascade's real per-iteration
compute cost (the surrogate is refit and the linkage tree rebuilt once per active level rather than once
total), this mixed, dataset-dependent evidence did not warrant adoption; it is left as a candidate for a
dedicated, better-powered follow-up (Conclusions). The remaining seven -- population truncation, stall
recovery, donor provenance, and all four $\kappa$/threshold spot-checks -- showed no significant difference
anywhere in the 112 comparisons across them, and were kept exactly as designed: nondominated-sort truncation,
reinjection-based stall recovery, $\mathcal{H}_t$-only donor provenance, and the original
$\kappa=2\lceil\log_2(n)\rceil$/zero-threshold defaults. The $\kappa$/threshold spot-checks are single-axis
snapshots, not the full joint grid; the dedicated joint sweep is reported separately, below.

Adopting analytic cost is not free of a cost of its own, however, and reporting it without this second
finding would be misleading by omission. Section~\ref{sec:results}'s Surrogate quality paragraph reports
that acceptance-gate precision collapsed from $0.672$ under the pre-adoption default to $0.497$ under the
now-adopted one -- a real regression on that specific measure, not a rounding artefact. A follow-up
investigation traced this directly, on matched seeds, rather than inferring it from aggregate statistics
alone, and ruled out four candidate explanations in turn: it is not a logging or selection artefact (the
telescoped $f_1$ estimate for a given candidate is byte-identical whether or not analytic cost is enabled,
confirmed by direct trace -- analytic cost only ever touches $f_2$); it is not accumulated telescoping error
from longer surrogate-only chains (mean accepted chain length is, if anything, shorter under the new default
across five matched seeds, $4.05$ vs.\ $4.65$, and the degradation is present at every chain length, not
concentrated in long chains); it is not a worse-fitting surrogate (in-sample $R^2$ on the model's own
training pairs is essentially unchanged, $0.85$--$0.99$ either way, with zero negative-$R^2$ iterations in
either condition); and it is not that promoted candidates sit farther from the training distribution
(distance to the training centroid and to the nearest training pair are both statistically indistinguishable
between the two defaults, and the degradation is uniform across near and far candidates alike). What does
explain it: because $C^*$ selection is nondomination in $(\hat{f}_1, f_2)$ (Deduplication,
Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}), and every logged prediction is by construction a predicted
improvement (a modification only reaches the log once accepted, Surrogate quality above), the quantity this
metric actually reports is the acceptance gate's precision -- and once $f_2$ genuinely varies per candidate
rather than being inherited as a near-constant, the gate starts promoting candidates on the strength of a
good $f_2$ as often as a confident $f_1$ prediction, which a direct sweep-by-sweep trace confirms: the two
defaults' evaluated-genotype sequences, identical through the bootstrap phase, diverge as early as the
fourth sweep-based decision on a matched seed. Fixed-budget hypervolume, the metric this decision was
actually made on, still favours analytic cost despite this (Fixed-budget summary, above) -- the two
outcomes are real and simultaneously true, and the full account of why a lower-precision gate coexists with
a better downstream result is left as an open point (Conclusions).

\paragraph{Surrogate error accumulation ($\kappa$ and acceptance threshold) sensitivity.} The joint
$\kappa \times$ acceptance-threshold grid (\texttt{configs/experiment/kappa\_threshold\_sweep.yaml}: $\kappa
\in \{1, \lceil\log_2 n\rceil, 2\lceil\log_2 n\rceil, \infty\}$, threshold $\in \{0, \epsilon, 2\epsilon\}$,
$\epsilon = 0.01$, full cross product, $R=30$) has since completed across all four search spaces and budget
tiers via \texttt{scripts/run\_kappa\_sensitivity.py}. Two results, neither favourable to retuning either
knob as a lever: fixed-budget hypervolume (own-run reference point) varies by only $0.08$--$0.68\%$ relative
spread across the full 12-cell grid per search space at budget~350 -- effectively flat -- and surrogate rank
correlation (Spearman, predicted vs.\ true $\delta$) is modestly positive everywhere ($0.14$--$0.41$
median across cells) but does not track the per-dataset outcome ranking (Fixed-budget summary, above):
NAS-HPO-Bench-II has the \emph{highest} rank correlation of the four search spaces ($0.29$--$0.41$) despite
the worst fixed-budget outcome, echoing the acceptance-gate-precision investigation's own conclusion that
aggregate surrogate-quality metrics do not explain the per-dataset pattern (Design decisions, below).
Separately, pooling this same completed sweep's diagnostics shows the mixing-driven (surrogate-guided)
share of the budget is itself essentially invariant to $\kappa$ and threshold -- $6.0$--$7.1\%$ median at
budget~350 in every one of the 12--16 cells per search space, matching the headline \texttt{p3net} arm's own
default-configuration share almost exactly (Diagnostics: population-pyramid bootstrap share, below) -- so
neither knob is an available fix for that finding either.

\paragraph{Diagnostics: genotype duplication.} Figure~\ref{fig:duplication-rate} shows mean proposal-time
duplication rate per method, pooled across all four search spaces, all four budgets, and all seeds. The
result still complicates rather than confirms the hypothesis Introduction and Related Work raise, that a
dependency-aware search operator should propose fewer duplicate genotypes than one blind to variable
structure: P3-alone ($0.734$) and P3+absolute-regressor ($0.409$) remain the two highest-duplication methods
in the grid, both P3-engine arms without the relative surrogate, while NSGA-Net ($0.038$), NSGANetV2
($0.046$), MO-BOHB ($0.0005$), random search ($0.0001$), and TPE ($0.087$) all sit at or near zero and
SH-EMOA sits at $0.234$. P3-alone-pop20 ($0.448$) barely improves on plain P3-alone despite doubling its
population to match every other baseline -- population size alone is a weak lever on duplication here.
P3-alone-pop40 ($0.187$), at four times the original population, does substantially better, but is still
roughly nine times P3Net's own rate ($0.021$, still the lowest or tied-lowest in the grid among the
P3-engine and NSGA-II-engine arms). Population size measurably helps, but even a fourfold increase does not
close the gap to what the relative, linkage-aware surrogate achieves on top of the \emph{original},
unenlarged population -- reinforcing, on a second and independent axis from hypervolume, that suppressing
duplicate proposals here is substantially a surrogate effect rather than a population-size effect the
original \texttt{p3\_alone.yaml}'s smaller population happened to obscure. Read together with the
fixed-budget summary above, where P3Net significantly beats plain P3-alone on three of four search spaces,
the duplication-rate gap is a plausible part of that mechanism on those search spaces: a duplicate proposal
wastes a sweep step without changing the population, so suppressing far more of them, as P3Net does, should
help regardless of channel -- though NAS-HPO-Bench-II, where the ablation win disappears and inverts at the
highest budget (Fixed-budget summary, above), is a reminder that a lower duplication rate does not by itself
guarantee a better fixed-budget outcome.

\paragraph{Diagnostics: population-pyramid bootstrap share.} A follow-up investigation, prompted by neither
the variance/IQR nor the acceptance-gate-precision account above cleanly explaining the per-dataset ranking
pattern, measured what fraction of \texttt{p3net}'s own evaluation budget is spent on genuinely
surrogate-guided optimal-mixing proposals versus uniform-random population bootstrap. \texttt{Pyramid}
(\texttt{search\_engines.p3.pyramid}) grows levels geometrically ($\text{size} = \text{growth\_factor}^{k}$,
i.e.\ $2, 4, 8, \dots, 256$ at the default \texttt{growth\_factor}$=2$); in its originally-diagnosed form it
started every new level from an \emph{empty} population filled only by uniform random sampling, and grew a
new level as soon as the current one failed once to strictly Pareto-dominate its own incumbent -- a
documented simplification (only the newest level is ever swept; Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}).
Because geometric growth makes each new level larger than the sum of every level before it, this
structurally forced the uniform-random-bootstrap share of the total budget to \emph{rise}, not fall, as the
budget grew, in the originally-diagnosed version: instrumented replay on NAS-HPO-Bench-II measured
$81.6\%\to93.0\%$ bootstrap share from budget~50 to~350. A redesign was implemented in response -- newly
grown levels are now warm-started from $\mathcal{H}_t$'s current nondominated front rather than starting
empty, and the strict-Pareto-dominance stall trigger is replaced by a hypervolume-contribution criterion
(Conclusions) -- and the numbers below, and every table in this section, reflect the full $R=30$ grid
re-run under that fix (2026-08-18), not the originally-diagnosed pre-fix behaviour.

Under the fix, the same NAS-HPO-Bench-II budget sweep now reads $68.6\%\to77.4\%\to85.6\%\to91.0\%$
(budgets $50,100,200,350$) -- still rising with budget, since the fix does not change the geometric growth
rule itself, but consistently lower at every tier than the pre-fix curve. Cross-checked at budget~350 across
all four search spaces: $90.3\%$ (JAHS-Bench-201/CIFAR-10), $91.7\%$ (Colorectal-Histology), $92.4\%$
(Fashion-MNIST), $91.0\%$ (NAS-HPO-Bench-II) -- down from the pre-fix $93$--$94\%$ uniformly, a real,
consistent, but modest reduction. The behavioural consequence, even under the fix:
\textbf{P3Net is still not statistically distinguishable from \texttt{random\_search} in any of the sixteen
(search-space, budget) cells} in the Fixed-budget summary above (every \texttt{random\_search} row already
shows \texttt{reject\_null=no}; Cliff's $\delta$ against \texttt{random\_search} is $\leq 0.25$ in magnitude
everywhere). Consequently P3Net's own margin against each of its ten baselines still tracks that baseline's
own margin against \texttt{random\_search} almost exactly (Spearman $\rho=0.945$, $n=144$, $p=4.7\times
10^{-71}$, across every search-space $\times$ budget $\times$ baseline combination) -- essentially unchanged
from the pre-fix $\rho=0.962$, since the mechanism (geometric growth outpacing any fixed-size fix to the
transition rule) is untouched by the redesign, only its severity is. This also reframes the ablation-family
wins reported above (Fixed-budget summary; Design decisions, below): \texttt{p3\_alone} and its
population-size variants use a \emph{fixed}, not growing, population and fail differently -- by stagnating
in a small, repeatedly-revisited population rather than by defaulting to broad random sampling (P3-alone
sweep-completion diagnostic, below) -- so P3Net's measured advantage over them is not sufficient evidence,
on its own, that the relative surrogate is what drives it; it is at least partly consistent with broad,
unguided exploration beating a stalled, narrow one. A joint $\kappa$/acceptance-threshold sensitivity sweep (Surrogate error accumulation, above), run against the
pre-fix pyramid, ruled out retuning either knob as an available fix. A further, literature-grounded
strengthening attempt was tried, reverted after a severe regression, and is left as the natural next
candidate (Conclusions).

A broader check across the full grid puts P3Net's own record in context. Repeating the identical
per-cell Holm-corrected comparison, but against \texttt{random\_search} as the reference arm instead of
P3Net (160 method $\times$ search-space $\times$ budget cells, the ten non-\texttt{random\_search} arms
corrected against it within each cell exactly as P3Net is corrected against its ten baselines elsewhere in
this section), $54$ of $160$ ($34\%$) reject the null under the fixed pyramid -- down from the pre-fix
$67/160$ ($42\%$), and failing to separate from random search in \emph{some} cells remains common across
this grid, not unique to P3Net. Per arm, the counts now range from SH-EMOA (12 of 16 cells) and P3-alone (11
of 16) down through NSGANetV2 (8), P3-alone-pop20 (7), NSGA-Net and P3-alone-pop40 (6 each), and TPE (3, down
from the pre-fix 5), to MO-BOHB (1 of 16, consistent with its own weak-separation account above), and
\textbf{P3+absolute-regressor and P3Net (0 of 16 each)}. The P3+absolute-regressor count is the single
largest mover in this table: it stood at 10 of 16 under its pre-fix, flat-population engine (Diagnostics:
surrogate representational capacity, above), and collapses to 0 of 16 once it shares P3Net's own
bootstrap-dominated \texttt{Pyramid} engine -- direct, mechanistic evidence that its apparent pre-fix
strength against random search was substantially an artefact of its \emph{different, non-Pyramid} population
management, not of the absolute-regressor surrogate it actually tests. Failing to separate from random
search in \emph{every} cell tested is now shared by P3Net and P3+absolute-regressor -- the two arms that run
\texttt{Pyramid} -- and nearly so by MO-BOHB; every arm that does \emph{not} run \texttt{Pyramid} still beats
random search significantly at least a fifth of the time.

\paragraph{Diagnostics: surrogate representational capacity.} A separate hypothesis from the same
investigation, orthogonal to \texttt{Pyramid}'s growth rule above: whether $\hat{\delta}_F$'s own model class
can represent what P3's linkage-guided crossover proposes. \texttt{RelativeLinkageAwareSurrogate.fit}
(\texttt{p3net.surrogates.relative\_linkage\_aware}) trains \texttt{sklearn.LinearRegression} on
$X = \mathrm{onehot}(x) \oplus \mathrm{onehot}(x')$, $y = f_1(x) - f_1(x')$; a linear model on concatenated
one-hot endpoints can only represent $\hat{\delta}_F(x,x') = w \cdot \mathrm{onehot}(x) - w \cdot
\mathrm{onehot}(x')$ -- an antisymmetric, strictly \emph{additive} function of the genotype coordinates,
with no term for how two simultaneously changed coordinates jointly affect the outcome, confirmed
empirically ($\mathrm{corr}(w_{\text{left}}, -w_{\text{right}}) = 1.0000$ exactly, 40/40 real fits pooled
across 10 seeds and all four search spaces). The identical model class -- \texttt{sklearn.LinearRegression}
over a plain one-hot encoding, no interaction terms -- is also what \texttt{AbsoluteRegressorSurrogate} uses
for both the P3+absolute-regressor ablation and NSGANetV2 (\texttt{scripts/run\_experiment.py::
build\_method} wires \texttt{model\_factory=LinearRegression} identically for all three). This limitation is
therefore shared by every surrogate-based arm in the grid, not specific to the relative formulation, and so
cannot by itself explain a difference \emph{between} them. A further complication for reading the
P3Net-vs-P3+absolute-regressor numbers reported above as isolating the surrogate alone: at the time that grid
was run, \texttt{methods.p3\_absolute.P3Absolute} did not reuse \texttt{Pyramid} -- it swept a single
fixed-size population of 20 every call, with no geometric growth and none of the bootstrap-dominance
pathology above (Diagnostics: population-pyramid bootstrap share) -- so the gap between the two arms above
mixes a surrogate-formulation difference with a population-management difference, not the surrogate alone as
the Baselines paragraph frames it. \texttt{P3Absolute} has since been rewritten to share P3Net's own Pyramid
engine exactly (Conclusions), closing this specific confound; the full grid has been re-run under it
(2026-08-18) and Tables~\ref{tab:fixed-budget-jahs}--\ref{tab:fixed-budget-nashpo} above already reflect the
shared-engine numbers, not the earlier flat-population ones. The headline result, stated here rather than
left implicit: with the population-engine confound removed, P3Net vs.\ P3+absolute-regressor reaches
significance in $0/16$ cells, in either direction (P3+abs.\ regressor rows above) -- once isolated, this grid
shows no measurable advantage of the relative surrogate over the absolute regressor (Conclusions).

What it can plausibly explain is why any of the three surrogate-based arms' benefit over their no-surrogate
counterparts (P3-alone, P3-alone-pop20/40, NSGA-Net) is inconsistent across datasets. P3's own crossover
restricts variation to linkage subsets specifically because the linkage tree has detected variables whose
\emph{joint} effect on fitness is not the sum of their individual effects -- protecting exactly those
detected groups from disruption is the stated purpose of linkage-guided crossover (Related Work); a purely
additive landscape would need no linkage tree at all, since a single-coordinate hill-climber would already
find the optimum. Scoring exactly those linkage-guided, multi-coordinate proposals with a model that is
mathematically blind to any interaction among the coordinates it groups together is a structural tension in
the current design, not merely a hyperparameter choice. A held-out-CV measure of ``architecture-involving
epistasis'' ($R^2$ gained by adding second-order interactions touching at least one architecture edge, over
a main-effects-only model of $f_1$) is flat in total ($0.40$--$0.50$) across all four search spaces but moves
in non-overlapping ranges, in exactly the order of where P3Net's win over its own no-surrogate ablations is
strongest to weakest: Fashion-MNIST $-0.020$ (near-perfectly additive; P3Net wins at every budget), CIFAR-10
$+0.034$, Colorectal-Histology $+0.102$, NAS-HPO-Bench-II $+0.250$ (P3Net's ablation wins disappear and
invert at budget~350) -- the same ordering the population-pyramid finding above already partly explains,
offered here as a second, structural and independently-motivated account rather than a competing one. $98\%$
of real P3Net moves touch an architecture edge, so this misspecification is the dominant, not a marginal,
error mode for this surrogate. Whether this specifically, rather than the bootstrap-dominance mechanism
above, is what destroys the ablation-family win on the highest-epistasis datasets was not tested
interventionally (e.g.\ by holding the pyramid fixed and swapping only the model class) and is flagged as
unverified; Conclusions returns to it as a second, independent candidate direction alongside the pyramid
redesign.

The candidate interaction-feature fix for this representational limit (Conclusions) is unit-tested against a
constructed non-additive pattern (capacity gain confirmed directly) and run on the full grid as
\texttt{p3net\_surrogate\_interactions}. Against the Phase-2-fixed \texttt{p3net} baseline it reaches
significance in $0/16$ cells, with small, sign-inconsistent effect sizes. The added capacity is real, but
this benchmark family's landscapes evidently do not carry
enough exploitable interaction structure, at this scale, for it to change search outcomes -- consistent with
the account (Related Work, Conclusions) that P3Net's mechanism needs more architectural complexity than these
four benchmarks offer before its advantage can show.

This also bears on how $\hat{\delta}_F$ compares to the two search-time, linkage-aware surrogates it is
positioned against in Related Work. Bartnik's absolute regressor is deliberately not restricted to a linear
model: it is fit as one of several nonlinear-capable families (SVR, MLP, random forest, or gradient
boosting)~\citep{bartnik2026evolutionary}, so an interaction between two genotype coordinates is
representable there regardless of whether her linkage mechanism routes it explicitly. eLyMPuS does not face
this specific failure mode either, but for a different, more structural reason: it is not a regression over
a concatenated encoding at all, but a discrete better/worse/ambiguous comparison tied directly to its own
recursive linkage discovery, under a monotonicity assumption P3Net's own adaptation explicitly declines to
import (Related Work)~\citep{przewozniczek2026lympus}. $\hat{\delta}_F$'s specific combination -- linear, and
fit over the flat concatenated encoding rather than routed through the linkage tree's own subset structure
-- is accordingly closer to what CS-GOMEA's authors already flag as an open problem in their own,
differently-shaped surrogate: a model that does not exploit linkage tree information in its own
construction~\citep{dushatskiy2019csgomea}.

\paragraph{Scope, and what would extend it.} The comparisons above hold within a single, shared cell search
space and small-scale image classification, across four datasets (Related Work), a scope this project's own
background research and the wider NAS benchmarking literature both document as a real limit on how far any
single-benchmark result generalises~\citep{yang2020nasfrustratinglyhard,mehta2022nasbenchsuite,
lopes2023nasbenchdesign,tu2022nasbench360,duan2021transnasbench101}. The dataset extension this project
carried out within that shared family -- running JAHS-Bench-201's Colorectal-Histology and Fashion-MNIST
datasets alongside its CIFAR-10 default -- is itself informative about that limit, and the honest reading is
mixed rather than confirmatory. The core surrogate-vs.-no-surrogate mechanism holds most completely on
Fashion-MNIST (Fixed-budget summary, above: P3Net beats every ablation-family arm at multiple budgets,
including the absolute-regressor variant, the largest and cleanest margin in the grid), holds partially on
CIFAR-10, narrows on Colorectal-Histology, and disappears -- inverting at the highest budget -- on
NAS-HPO-Bench-II, the one benchmark built from measured rather than surrogate-modelled data. Extending
within this benchmark family did not produce a uniformly stronger case for the mechanism; it produced real,
measured heterogeneity, which is itself evidence worth having rather than a null result to discount.

Two observations, unaffected by that within-family heterogeneity because they concern a different axis --
structural complexity and dimensionality, not dataset identity -- are still consistent with, though do not
by themselves establish, the hypothesis that the relative surrogate's advantage would be at least as large
on a bigger or more structurally complex search space than any tested here. First, the surrogate's value on
its best-case search spaces is a real, replicated effect obtained on a search space Lopes et al.\ document as
comparatively small and unstructured among NAS benchmarks~\citep{lopes2023nasbenchdesign} -- toward the
easier end of what the P3/GOMEA lineage has been validated on, not the harder end where this lineage's own
literature reports its advantage over generic search actually growing (Related Work). Second, the
duplication-rate gap (Diagnostics, above) separates P3Net from every non-linkage-aware method by roughly an
order of magnitude on this six-edge cell encoding, consistently across all four datasets regardless of
whether the fixed-budget comparison favours P3Net on that dataset or not; a larger or deeper genotype, with
more higher-order dependencies for a linkage tree to actually exploit, is exactly the regime in which this
method family's own combinatorial validation literature reports that gap widening further, not
narrowing~\citep{goldman2015fast,qiao2024gomearuntime,dushatskiy2019csgomea}. Neither observation licenses a
claim that P3Net would outperform its baselines on a larger search space or a different task domain --
establishing that would require actually running the comparison there, which this study does not do, and the
dataset-level heterogeneity just reported is itself a reason for caution about how far any such extrapolation
should be trusted -- but together they remain a specific, measured reason, tempered rather than strengthened
by what extending within this family actually showed, that testing a larger or more structurally complex
search space is a reasoned next step and not merely a possible one (Conclusions).
