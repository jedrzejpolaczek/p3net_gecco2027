\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports surrogate quality, convergence under fixed evaluation budgets, Pareto front and
hypervolume comparisons, and a fixed-budget summary table (median, IQR, corrected $p$~values, effect sizes),
in that order. The experimental setup and protocol are detailed first.

\subsection*{Experimental Setup}

The protocol must not allow P3Net to be read as ``P3 plus Pareto filtering.'' It states explicitly which
selection mechanics NSGA-II contributes to the baseline, what P3 replaces (the variation operator and the
proposal distribution), and, separately from the choice of search engine, which surrogate variant is used
(linkage aware $\hat{\delta}_F$ vs.\ an absolute regressor), so that gains can be attributed to the correct
component rather than conflated.

\paragraph{Benchmark and search space.} P3Net targets the joint architecture and hyperparameter search
setting rather than an architecture only space. Rather than resolving the Category~1 vs.\ Category~2
question by picking a single benchmark, both are reported side by side, deliberately. The primary
benchmark is JAHS-Bench-201~\citep{bansal2022jahsbench}, a surrogate benchmark over a joint search space of
ten dimensions (six categorical architecture edges plus four hyperparameters, including continuous learning
rate and weight decay), fourteen when JAHS-Bench-201's four additional fidelity dimensions are counted
alongside it, per the source's own ``14-dimensional search and fidelity space'' figure, across three
datasets; it queries a learned surrogate over its full mixed
categorical/continuous search space without live training, so no enumerable oracle front exists (see
Metrics, below).
NAS-HPO-Bench-II~\citep{hirose2021nashpobenchii}, a fixed grid lookup table over combinations of
architecture and hyperparameters, is reported alongside it as the Category~1 counterpart: within its exhaustively tabulated range (up to 12 training epochs; Section~\ref{sec:related-work} confirms $r_K$ is fixed at this level, not the benchmark's separate 200-epoch surrogate extrapolation), an
exact oracle front is enumerable, enabling the Inverted Generational Distance plus (IGD+) on this benchmark specifically. Any result that holds on
both benchmarks is reported as the primary finding; a result that holds on only one is reported as
specific to that benchmark and not over generalised.

\paragraph{Baselines.} NSGANetV2 (NSGA-II with an absolute regressor surrogate) as the primary baseline;
NSGANetV2 extended to the joint setting with an unconstrained, real-valued encoding of $\Theta$ as an
additional control baseline isolating the effect of discretisation itself, rather than of the search engine
or surrogate (Fairness controls);
NSGA-Net (NSGA-II with no surrogate, every candidate fully evaluated) as the engine-only ablation on the
NSGA-II side, symmetric to the P3-only ablation below; P3 alone, without any surrogate, as the engine-only
ablation on the P3 side; P3 with an absolute regressor surrogate (in the style of NSGANetV2) as an ablation
isolating only the surrogate, separating the contribution of the linkage aware design from the contribution
of the P3 engine itself; SH-EMOA (a multi objective evolutionary algorithm built on SMS-EMOA) and
Multi-Objective Bayesian Optimization Hyperband (MO-BOHB), the baselines established for this exact joint
setting on these same two benchmarks~\citep{guerreroviu2021bagofbaselines}, included because, unlike
NSGANetV2, they were never imported from an architecture only setting and require no such extension; random
search and the Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) as sanity baselines.
This grid separates the engine and surrogate contributions as far as the design permits, not as a full
orthogonal factorial: the relative, linkage aware surrogate $\hat{\delta}_F$ is defined only given a linkage
tree, so an NSGA-II-plus-relative-surrogate cell is not constructible, and any gap attributed to ``the
surrogate'' via the P3-only vs.\ P3Net comparison should be read against this asymmetry.

Without a surrogate, P3 alone follows the canonical optimal-mixing acceptance rule: every proposed
modification within a sweep is gated by a \emph{real} evaluation of $f_1$, exactly as step~3 of the P3Net
loop (Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}) would be if $\hat{\delta}_F$ were replaced by $f_1$ itself, and as in the original
P3 algorithm's own hill-climbing and cross-level mixing steps~\citep{goldman2014parameterless}. Under the
$\{50,\, 100,\, 200\}$-evaluation budgets used here (Budgets, below), this is expected to exhaust most or
all of the budget within a small number of sweeps: for a joint genotype with dozens of linkage subsets, a
single parent's full sweep alone can approach the smallest tier. This is not a flaw in the ablation but its
point: if P3 alone collapses under this budget, that result directly motivates pairing P3 with a cheap
surrogate in the first place, which is what the central question (Section~\ref{sec:introduction}) actually compares. We
report the number of full sweeps completed within budget for this ablation specifically, alongside its
hypervolume, so a weak result reads as budget starvation rather than as a failure of the search engine
itself.

\paragraph{Surrogate error accumulation ($\kappa$ and acceptance threshold).} The main comparison above
fixes $\kappa$ at its default value, eLyMPuS's own $2\lceil\log_2(n)\rceil$-step bound (Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}),
and the step-3 acceptance rule at its default zero threshold ($\hat{\delta}_F \geq 0$). Both are swept
jointly in the sensitivity analysis: $\kappa$ over a small grid (e.g.\ $\{1,\, \lceil\log_2(n)\rceil,\,
2\lceil\log_2(n)\rceil,\, \infty\}$), and the acceptance threshold over a small margin grid (e.g.\ $\{0,\,
\epsilon,\, 2\epsilon\}$ for a fixed $\epsilon > 0$), with P3Net otherwise unchanged, reporting fixed budget
hypervolume and surrogate rank correlation as a function of both.

\paragraph{Fairness controls.} Identical genotype encoding, decoding function $D$, validity handling,
full evaluation budget, seed policy, stopping rule, and deduplication cache (Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}) across all
compared methods, so that no method's apparent efficiency benefits from infrastructure the others lack. In
particular, the discretisation of continuous hyperparameter coordinates (Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}) is applied
identically to every baseline: NSGA-II/NSGANetV2 search the same discretised $\Theta$ as P3Net rather than
the unconstrained real-valued encoding a joint-setting extension of NSGA-II/NSGANetV2 would otherwise use,
so that any gain is attributable to the search engine and surrogate rather than to a difference in genotype
resolution. This equalises resolution but is not necessarily neutral: discretisation removes the real-valued
crossover on $\Theta$ that such an extension would otherwise use, while costing P3 nothing, since P3 operates
on a categorical genotype regardless. This asymmetry is bounded empirically
rather than merely acknowledged: NSGANetV2 in its native continuous encoding is included as an additional
control baseline (Baselines, above), so any P3Net advantage can be checked specifically against the
discretised-NSGANetV2 comparison, rather than resting on the discretised comparison alone.

\paragraph{Budgets, seeds, stopping.} Three full evaluation budget tiers, $\{50,\, 100,\, 200\}$ full
evaluations, doubling at each step so convergence trends read on a natural, geometric scale. Each tier is
anchored to a figure actually used in the surrounding literature rather than chosen arbitrarily: the middle
tier matches JAHS-Bench-201's own suggested evaluation protocol of approximately 100 full evaluations, with
a minimum of 10 seeds~\citep{bansal2022jahsbench}; the top tier stays at or below the 350 full evaluations
NSGANetV2 itself uses to reach its reported results~\citep{lu2020nsganetv2}, so no tier exceeds the regime
in which the primary baseline was originally shown to work well. This also keeps every tier below
NAS-HPO-Bench-II's own convention of 500 trials for benchmarking search
algorithms~\citep{hirose2021nashpobenchii}, a deliberate choice, not an oversight: the central question
concerns a \emph{limited} budget (Section~\ref{sec:introduction}), so all three tiers stress-test a regime tighter than either
benchmark's own default, on both benchmarks alike. $R = 10$ independent runs per method with a fixed, published
seed list, matching JAHS-Bench-201's recommended minimum~\citep{bansal2022jahsbench}; identical
initialization scheme across methods; a stopping rule covering both the evaluation budget and, if the
search space is small enough to converge prematurely, an exploration collapse criterion analogous to the
one Bartnik~\citep{bartnik2026evolutionary} needed to add for spaces at the scale of NAS-Bench-201.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Fixed budget hypervolume and, only if the search space admits an enumerable oracle
front, IGD+; otherwise hypervolume relative to the best known front is used in its place \emph{(TODO: define this front precisely, e.g.\ as the union of all points evaluated by any compared method across all runs)}. Surrogate quality
is reported separately as rank correlation (or pairwise comparison accuracy, if the surrogate is relational)
between predicted and true values as a function of $|\mathcal{H}_t|$.

\paragraph{Statistical plan.} The comparison set is P3Net against each of the other nine arms (Baselines,
above), per benchmark and per budget tier, not every pairwise combination among all arms. Paired Wilcoxon
signed rank tests are run per comparison in this set, with Holm--Bonferroni correction applied across it,
and an effect size (e.g.\ Cliff's delta) reported alongside every corrected $p$ value. This is stricter than the uncorrected comparisons in the closest prior
work~\citep{bartnik2026evolutionary}, which report no effect size, and the difference is treated as a
deliberate methodological improvement, not an oversight to reconcile.

\paragraph{Diagnostics.} Genotype duplication rate and archive turnover over the course of the search,
motivated directly by the genotype-to-phenotype redundancy documented in NSGA-Net (Section~\ref{sec:introduction})~\citep{lu2019nsganet},
measured under the identical joint genotype encoding fixed for every compared method (Fairness controls),
so that any difference in duplication rate reflects the search operator rather than a difference in
encoding. The deduplication cache (Section~\ref{sec:proposed-optimizer}) is applied identically to every compared method as
part of the shared evaluation harness (Fairness controls), so the measured duplication rate reflects what
each search operator proposes, not an artifact of asymmetric caching. This rate is counted at proposal
time, for every candidate a search operator generates, whether or not it is later intercepted by the cache,
not at evaluation time, so it remains informative even though the cache itself suppresses re-evaluation of
anything it counts. Convergence curves; Pareto front
progression; stability across seeds.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Every cached evaluation is keyed by genotype, experiment type, and a protocol
version string covering every setting that affects the recorded value, so that a change in any of them
invalidates stale cache entries automatically, following the pattern used
by~\citep{bartnik2026evolutionary}.
