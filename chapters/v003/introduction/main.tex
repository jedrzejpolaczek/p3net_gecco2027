\section{Introduction}

Neural architecture search explores large, discrete spaces of candidate network designs, and assessing any single candidate against the true objective, whether by training it from scratch, through weight sharing, or by querying a benchmark built for this purpose, is the dominant cost of the search. Treating architecture search and hyperparameter tuning as two separate stages, first the architecture under a fixed or default hyperparameter setting, then tuning hyperparameters for the winning architecture, is common practice. Zela et al.\ \citep{zela2018towards} show it to be unreliable on two counts: architecture and hyperparameters interact, so the best architecture under one hyperparameter setting need not be the best under another; and the common shortcut of ranking architectures after only a few training epochs correlates poorly with their ranking at the full training budget. Separating the two stages compounds this risk twice over. Searching both jointly, rather than sequentially, avoids that compounding, at the cost of a further enlarged search space on top of an already expensive one.

NSGA-Net \citep{lu2019nsganet} established evolutionary search directly over network architectures, training every candidate to completion. NSGANetV2 \citep{lu2020nsganetv2} showed that pairing the same evolutionary engine with a learned accuracy predictor lets most candidates be screened cheaply, reserving full evaluation for a small, promising subset; this surrogate assisted pattern is now a popular approach in evolutionary NAS, though systematic surveys of the field do not single it out as the one dominant paradigm (Related Work).

NSGA-Net's own experiments hint that this hidden dependency structure is not innocuous: 60--80\% of generated genotypes decode to duplicate architectures as the number of nodes grows \citep{lu2019nsganet}, evidence that variables in the encoding interact in ways an operator blind to that structure can only exploit by accident, if at all. Decoding redundancy by itself is not an unsolved engineering problem: canonicalising isomorphic cells when building a benchmark (e.g.\ NAS-Bench-101 \citep{ying2019nasbench101}) and approximate duplicate detection during search (NSGA-Net itself, and P3Net through its own deduplication cache, Proposed Optimizer) have been standard practice since 2019. What matters is that the same redundancy occurs unchanged in the NAS-Bench-201 cell space \citep{dong2020nasbench201} that JAHS-Bench-201 \citep{bansal2022jahsbench} is built on, one of this paper's two primary benchmarks (Results): of 15,625 raw encodings, only 6,466 (${\sim}41\%$) are topologically unique, an ${\sim}59\%$ duplication rate, and NAS-Bench-201's own authors state they built it ``without considering isomorphism.'' What remains open is therefore not the existence of duplicates, but the narrower question of whether a dependency-aware search operator generates fewer of them at proposal time, rather than merely detecting them more cheaply after the fact. A separate line of work, linkage learning evolutionary algorithms such as GOMEA \citep{thierens2011optimal} and the Parameter-less Population Pyramid (P3) \citep{goldman2014parameterless}, developed largely independently of this NSGA-II-based NAS lineage, targets exactly this: it learns which genotype variables interact and protects these groups during crossover, rather than treating the genotype as an unstructured encoding, in principle avoiding the kind of redundant, duplicate-generating search that NSGA-Net's own numbers document. Duplicate generation and fitness-relevant variable interaction are related but distinct: the former is a property of the decoding map alone, the latter of how genotype variables jointly determine the objective. A dependency-aware operator targets the latter. Whether this also reduces the former, whether decoding redundancy in practice correlates with the linkage structure P3 discovers, is an empirical question, not an assumption; Results' duplication-rate diagnostic tests it directly under the joint genotype.

P3Net combines these two levers. It replaces NSGA-II with P3 as the search engine, so that crossover follows a linkage tree learned from the population rather than acting on individual variables, and pairs it with a surrogate that is relative and linkage aware: rather than regressing a candidate's absolute accuracy from its full encoding, it predicts the change in accuracy relative to a parent, restricted to the one linkage subset that was modified, in the spirit of eLyMPuS, a variant of LyMPuS \citep{przewozniczek2026lympus} that predicts relative fitness change along a linkage structure discovered incrementally rather than assumed known in advance (Related Work elaborates). Both the search engine and the surrogate are extended to the joint setting, where the genotype includes training hyperparameters such as learning rate and weight decay alongside architectural choices.

Central question and contribution: under a fixed, limited full evaluation budget, does P3's dependency aware search, paired with a linkage aware, relative surrogate, produce candidates with a better accuracy/computational-cost trade-off than NSGA-II's crowding distance selection paired with an absolute regressor surrogate, as in NSGANetV2? We test this in the joint architecture and hyperparameter setting. Matched ablations separate the two levers, search engine and surrogate, as far as the design permits; the relative, linkage aware surrogate is only defined given a linkage tree, so it cannot itself be paired with NSGA-II, and Results details this limit of the ablation design. We answer the question through P3Net: a linkage learning evolutionary algorithm paired with a relative, linkage aware surrogate for joint architecture and hyperparameter search, evaluated via a controlled comparison against NSGANetV2 and these matched ablations, under equal full evaluation budgets, on two joint benchmarks, JAHS-Bench-201 and NAS-HPO-Bench-II. The closest prior work already combines a surrogate assisted linkage learning algorithm with network search, on an architecture only space \citep{bartnik2026evolutionary}; Related Work details how P3Net's linkage aware relative surrogate, its direct comparison against NSGA-II/NSGANetV2, and its joint architecture and hyperparameter scope differ from that baseline.
