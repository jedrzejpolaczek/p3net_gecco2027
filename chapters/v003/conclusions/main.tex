\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We asked whether P3's dependency-aware variation, paired with a relative linkage-aware surrogate, gives a better
accuracy/cost trade-off than NSGA-II with an absolute surrogate for joint architecture and hyperparameter search
under a small evaluation budget. On the benchmarks tested it does not: P3Net never separates from uniform random
search, and it loses to SH-EMOA, TPE, NSGA-Net, and NSGANetV2 increasingly as budget grows.

\paragraph{What the diagnosis supports.} The strongest account is structural and sits in the search engine. P3's
geometric level growth forces the uniform-random bootstrap share of the budget upward with budget, and a warm
start lowers it only modestly because it leaves the growth rule intact. The account predicts exactly the two
patterns we see: behaviour indistinguishable from random search, and widening losses at larger budgets. It is
also the one account supported by an intervention rather than a correlation: the absolute-surrogate arm lost
all separation from random search once it was moved onto the same pyramid. Two further observations are
consistent with the null but not established as its cause. The relative surrogate is additive by construction
and its acceptance gate performs at chance, and the ordering of a measured interaction proxy across search spaces
matches the ordering of P3Net's ablation wins. Adding interaction features to the surrogate, however, did not
change search outcomes.

\paragraph{What the isolation experiments rule out.} Removing the hyperparameter dimension does not change the
result, so the null is not an artefact of the joint genotype this work introduces. A reconstruction of the closest
prior surrogate-assisted linkage-learning design, which avoids both geometric bootstrap and an additive
surrogate, reproduces the null on the same benchmark. What these two experiments leave open is whether the
limiting factor is the benchmark family itself or a property that both designs share. The cell space used here is
documented as small, redundant, and skewed toward good architectures~\citep{lopes2023nasbenchdesign}, and
den Ottelander et al.\ saw linkage learning separate from random search only on a larger, less redundant
space~\citep{denottelander2021localsearch}. That is consistent with a benchmark ceiling, but we have not shown that
no linkage-learning design can clear it on this family, and we do not claim so.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Results are confined to one cell-based architecture family and small-scale image
classification. JAHS-Bench-201 answers through a learned surrogate, so results there inherit its approximation
error; every arm queries the same surrogate, but its error could still correlate with the structure P3 exploits.
Hyperparameters are discretised for every arm, and the control bounding what that costs NSGANetV2 was not run.
The surrogate ablation changes regularisation as well as formulation. Several of P3Net's own design defaults --
computing $f_2$ fresh rather than inheriting it, nondominated-sort truncation, donor provenance, $\kappa$, and the
acceptance threshold -- were selected in a single-axis ablation round run before the pyramid redesign and were not
re-validated after it. The linkage tree uses pairwise mutual information, which may miss higher-order dependencies.

\paragraph{Future work.} Three directions follow directly. First, a pyramid whose growth is driven by the
available donor pool rather than by a fixed geometric schedule; our attempt to start mixing earlier with a
fixed constant regressed severely, because optimal mixing with donors degenerates without a real population.
Second, a surrogate that exploits the linkage tree in its own construction, the open problem CS-GOMEA's authors
already name~\citep{dushatskiy2019csgomea}, rather than a flat additive model. Third, and most decisive for the
question this paper leaves open, a larger or less redundant search space: den Ottelander et al.'s
result~\citep{denottelander2021localsearch} and the scale-dependence of this lineage on combinatorial
problems~\citep{goldman2015fast,qiao2024gomearuntime} both suggest that is where any advantage would first appear.
