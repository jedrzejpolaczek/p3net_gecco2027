\section{Related Work}

Evolutionary architecture search without surrogate assistance was pioneered by NSGA-Net \citep{lu2019nsganet}, which employs NSGA-II as the search engine for convolutional network architectures, with selection based on nondominated sorting and crowding distance. Every candidate in this approach undergoes full training, and the only mechanism accelerating convergence is the exploitation of search history through a Bayesian network. Notably, the authors document substantial redundancy from genotype to phenotype, reporting that 60--80\% of generated genotypes correspond to duplicate architectures as the number of nodes grows, which indicates a nontrivial, nonlinear dependency structure hidden within the architecture encoding.

A year later, NSGANetV2 \citep{lu2020nsganetv2} introduced an accuracy predictor refined online during the search, and replaced full training from scratch with fine tuning of weights inherited from a supernet, reducing the number of fully evaluated architectures to 350 (Table 2 of the original study), a substitution that trades training cost for a documented risk of rank disagreement between weight shared and independently trained accuracy \citep{yu2020evaluatingnas}. This scheme, combining NSGA-II with a learned surrogate, remains the dominant paradigm in multi objective NAS, as independently confirmed by three systematic literature surveys \citep{liu2023survey, white2023insights, ozcelik2026survey}. Further development in this direction has moved toward predictors based on ranking rather than regression, such as RankNet in MoSegNAS \citep{lu2022mosegnas}, which extends the NSGANetV2 approach to semantic segmentation, as well as toward a critical reassessment of zero cost proxies, whose documented weakness is unreliable discrimination among top ranked architectures.

In parallel, a separate family of evolutionary algorithms has developed that explicitly models structural dependencies between genotype variables, namely GOMEA \citep{thierens2011optimal} and the Parameter-less Population Pyramid (P3) \citep{goldman2014parameterless}. These algorithms build a linkage tree from statistical dependencies observed in the population and use it to guide crossover, protecting detected groups of dependent variables from being disrupted. P3 additionally counteracts the premature convergence typical of generational models: unlike NSGA-II, it does not discard previously found good solutions while introducing new diversity. Structurally, P3 replaces a single fixed-size population with an ordered pyramid of populations of growing size, adding a new level only once existing levels stop yielding improved solutions -- the property the name refers to as parameter-less, since no population size is chosen in advance \citep{goldman2014parameterless}. Building and promoting a single new solution up the pyramid canonically requires a real fitness evaluation at every accepted local improvement, not only at the end of the process; this detail matters directly for budget accounting under P3Net's small evaluation budgets (Results returns to it when discussing the surrogate-free P3 ablation).

It is worth noting that the population level, statistically learned linkage described above should not be confused with gray box optimization in the narrower sense established by Whitley, Chicano, and Goldman \citep{whitley2016graybox}, and used since within the GOMEA line of work, including its real-valued extension RV-GOMEA \citep{andreadis2024maxclique}, and the joint GOMEA library \citep{bouter2023library}. In that setting, the optimizer is given explicit access to the subfunctions composing the objective and exploits this access to perform cheap, partial fitness reevaluations after a localized variable change. The present work instead operates in the black box regime: NAS validation accuracy cannot be decomposed into locally recomputable subfunctions, so P3 here serves purely as a search engine aware of the genotype's dependency structure, while cost reduction is achieved through the learned surrogate discussed next.

As with NSGA-Net and NSGANetV2, the cost of full evaluation remains the central bottleneck for linkage learning evolutionary algorithms as well: each optimal mixing step proposes a partial variable change whose effect on the objective must, under the black box regime described above, be verified through a full fitness evaluation. Pairing this search engine with a cheap learned surrogate that screens candidates before committing to a full evaluation is therefore a natural extension.

Combining linkage learning algorithms with a learned surrogate has already been explored, though not in the NAS domain. CS-GOMEA \citep{dushatskiy2019csgomea} integrates GOMEA with a convolutional neural network acting as a surrogate, evaluated on synthetic combinatorial problems (Onemax, Trap functions, NK landscapes, HIFF); the authors explicitly identify the surrogate's failure to exploit linkage tree information in its own construction as an open research direction. Dushatskiy, Alderliesten, and Bosman \citep{dushatskiy2021novelsurrogateassistedevolutionaryalgorithm} integrate a surrogate directly with a P3 variant, the first such documented case, testing the approach on an expensive, real world partition based ensemble learning problem; notably, an SVR model outperformed a neural predictor in their experiments. The most recent work in this line, LyMPuS \citep{przewozniczek2026lympus}, introduces a surrogate integrated directly with the linkage discovery mechanism and verifies it experimentally in combination with P3, again on classical combinatorial benchmarks such as NK landscapes, Ising Spin Glasses, and Max3Sat. P3Net's surrogate builds specifically on eLyMPuS -- the ``empirical'' LyMPuS, introduced in the same work -- which replaces LyMPuS's assumption of a known true dependency structure with the incrementally and possibly incompletely discovered dependency structure available in a black-box setting, exactly the regime P3Net operates in; Proposed Optimizer details the adaptation.

Among applications of linkage learning algorithms directly to NAS that forgo a learned surrogate entirely, the only identified work is Tran, Truong, Vo, and Luong \citep{tran2023gomeanas}, combining GOMEA with Synaptic Flow, a training free metric computed analytically that requires no learning from prior evaluation outcomes. This constitutes a fundamentally different cost reduction mechanism than a learned surrogate, and, as discussed below, is distinct from Bartnik's \citep{bartnik2026evolutionary} surrogate assisted linkage learning applied to NAS.

Independently of linkage learning approaches, a narrower body of work has addressed the joint optimisation of network architecture and training hyperparameters, rather than treating hyperparameter tuning as a separate step following architecture search \citep{zela2018towards}. Standardised benchmarks for this setting have since been introduced, namely JAHS-Bench-201 \citep{bansal2022jahsbench}, a surrogate benchmark over a joint search space combining categorical and continuous variables, and NAS-HPO-Bench-II \citep{hirose2021nashpobenchii}, a fixed grid lookup table over combinations of architecture and hyperparameters. A systematic comparison of solvers on this setting \citep{guerreroviu2021bagofbaselines} evaluates standard baselines such as NSGA-II, Multi-Objective Bayesian Optimization Hyperband (MO-BOHB), and random search; none of the methods compared in this line of work is a linkage learning evolutionary algorithm.

Across this joint NAS+HPO literature, the hyperparameter half of the search space is consistently the training procedure, not the network's structural capacity. JAHS-Bench-201 \citep{bansal2022jahsbench} searches learning rate, weight decay, optimizer, activation function, and data augmentation as hyperparameters, while the number of cells and the channel width are instead treated as a separate fidelity axis alongside training epochs and input resolution, distinct from both the architecture and hyperparameter dimensions. NAS-HPO-Bench-II \citep{hirose2021nashpobenchii} varies only learning rate and batch size as hyperparameters, on top of a fixed cell topology search space, with training duration handled through a separate surrogate rather than as a searched variable. Zela et al.\ \citep{zela2018towards} likewise jointly optimise cell operations together with learning rate, weight decay, batch size, and regularisation, using training epochs only as the resource dimension of Hyperband rather than as a decision variable. P3Net follows the same convention: the genotype extension for the joint setting adds training procedure hyperparameters such as learning rate and weight decay, while structural capacity choices remain part of the architecture encoding proper. Bartnik \citep{bartnik2026evolutionary} sidesteps this design question entirely, since her genotype has no hyperparameter dimension at all: training procedure is fixed by the NAS-Bench-201 lookup table, and her own discussion acknowledges that the resulting conclusions describe that closed search space rather than NAS in general, a limitation that directly motivates evaluating on genuinely joint benchmarks such as those above.

The joint NAS+HPO literature surveyed above also differs in what supplies the ground truth for evaluation. Zela et al.\ \citep{zela2018towards} optimise the joint setting through BOHB on real training runs. NAS-HPO-Bench-II \citep{hirose2021nashpobenchii} and JAHS-Bench-201 \citep{bansal2022jahsbench}, by contrast, answer every query through a fixed lookup table or a learned surrogate respectively, without live training at evaluation time. We refer to benchmarks of the first kind, with a fixed, exhaustively enumerable lookup table over which an exact oracle Pareto front is therefore computable, as \emph{Category 1}, and to benchmarks of the second kind, which answer every query through a learned, continuous surrogate over the search space and admit no enumerable oracle front, as \emph{Category 2}; in this terminology, NAS-HPO-Bench-II is Category 1 and JAHS-Bench-201 is Category 2. P3Net's primary benchmarks are these same two, chosen for the reasons already established in this line of work and in the Category 1 NAS literature more broadly: a deterministic objective, a computable oracle front, and a bounded evaluation budget.

This benchmark level surrogate should not be conflated with the learned surrogate that P3Net, CS-GOMEA \citep{dushatskiy2019csgomea}, Dushatskiy et al.\ \citep{dushatskiy2021novelsurrogateassistedevolutionaryalgorithm}, and LyMPuS \citep{przewozniczek2026lympus} build during the search itself. JAHS-Bench-201's surrogate stands in for the objective function once, offline, before any search begins; the search time surrogate used by these linkage learning methods is instead fitted incrementally from candidates already evaluated, purely to decide which further candidates are worth a full query. The two roles are independent design choices, and P3Net's contribution concerns only the second, layered on top of whichever substrate answers the full evaluation.

Closest to the present work, Bartnik \citep{bartnik2026evolutionary} adapts SA-P3-GOMEA, a surrogate assisted linkage learning algorithm, to multi objective NAS on NAS-Bench-201, optimising validation accuracy against measured GPU energy consumption. The surrogate used there is an absolute regressor over the flat genotype (deterministic or probabilistic variants of SVR, MLP, random forest, or gradient boosting), the compared baselines are MO-LS and surrogate free MO-P3-GOMEA rather than NSGA-II or NSGANetV2, and the search space is the closed NAS-Bench-201 benchmark restricted to architecture only, with no training hyperparameters included in the genotype.

Three independent systematic surveys of the evolutionary NAS literature \citep{liu2023survey, white2023insights, ozcelik2026survey} do not list GOMEA, P3, or linkage learning more broadly as a distinct method category, indicating that this algorithm family remains outside the mainstream of the field. While the combination of a linkage learning EA with a learned surrogate has already been empirically validated, and Bartnik's work extends it to NAS, three aspects remain jointly unaddressed: a linkage aware, relative surrogate rather than an absolute regressor over the flat genotype; NSGA-II/NSGANetV2 as a directly compared baseline; and the joint search over architecture and hyperparameters, to which, to our knowledge, no linkage learning evolutionary algorithm has previously been applied.
